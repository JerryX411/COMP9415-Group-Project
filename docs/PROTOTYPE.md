# Voxel Relay - prototype 0.7

Local implementation: C++17, OpenGL 3.3, GLFW and the course starter's ImGui / shader / window / benchmark integration. The original assignment specification remains in the root README. Prototype features were developed with Codex assistance; this document does not attribute the implementation to either student.

## Play

Start `Craft.exe`, select a level and enter the station. Recover every core with **E**, then pass through the unlocked gate. Walking into a core does not collect it.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move relative to the camera |
| Mouse / wheel | Orbit / zoom |
| Hold either Shift | Sprint, 8.4 units/s instead of 5.2 |
| Space | Tap for a low jump; hold for full height |
| C | Toggle crouch: 2.6 units/s, no sprint, collider 0.95 tall (fits a one-voxel gap); sprint or jump cancels. At sprint speed it slides instead |
| E | Recover the nearest core, operate a relay or activate a checkpoint |
| Escape | Pause menu / resume |
| R / 1 / 2 | Restart / courtyard / corridors |
| F1 / F2 / F3 | Tools / overview camera / motion workshop |

Every gameplay key above (move, jump, sprint, crouch, interact, restart) can be rebound under **Settings**, on both the main menu and the pause menu. Select a row and press Enter or click it, then press the new key; Esc cancels. A key already in use swaps over to the other action, so no key is bound twice. Esc, Enter, F1-F3, 1 and 2 are reserved. **Reset to defaults** restores the table above. Bindings are saved to `voxel_relay_keys.ini` in the working directory and loaded at start-up. The HUD hints and E prompts show the bound keys. `--settings` captures the page.

Mouse sensitivity is 0.0016 radians per pixel (previously 0.0035). The courier has five armour cells. A valid hazard hit removes one cell and five seconds, launches the courier away, and blinks for 1.1 seconds. Immunity is per hazard: the same trap plate or moving hazard cannot hit again for 0.5 seconds, but a different hazard hits immediately, so a hit that knocks the courier onto another trap chains into a second hit (each one kicks the camera). A checkpoint reboot grants one second of full immunity. Static walls block movement. The fifth hit triggers shutdown, a collapse animation, fading lights and sparks; restart restores the entire level. Timer expiry also causes shutdown.

Core recovery is a 0.46-second reach animation with an energy tether. The core is awarded halfway through the animation, once only. A hit interrupts unfinished recovery. Switches use the same interaction key and temporarily disable their linked floor trap.

## Presentation update (0.7)

**Main menu.** The title screen is a white test chamber: flat ceramic wall panels with hairline seams, graphite floor plates, a column of dark panels, one shaft of light and drifting dust. The courier stands on a lit plinth, follows the cursor with its head and waves every few seconds with happy eyes. The menu itself is a single text list (Start, Level, Motion Lab, Quit) with a sliding highlight; W/S or the arrow keys select, A/D or left/right change level, Enter or a click confirms. Pause now dims the frozen level instead of switching scenes. The title list is Start, Level, Motion Lab, Settings, Quit. The title screen shows only the wordmark and the list: no header code, subtitle, item numbers or select/confirm key hints (the pause menu keeps them). Escape pauses play and offers Resume, Return to Checkpoint (to the entry point if none is active; keeps armour and clock), Restart Level, Settings and Main Menu.

**Menus.** The title menu is a slanted slab: a staggered VOXEL / RELAY wordmark, one-word items with a cyan selection blade, and a rotating reticle with a status tag around the courier. Results appear on a full-width band. The current look (palette, fonts, textures and motion) is described under Interface style below.

**Level transitions and crouch.** Restarting (R), switching levels (1 / 2, result-screen buttons, F1 tools) fades to black in 0.6 s. The level loads at full black and a sector title card holds for 1.8 s: the name types out behind a block cursor while a load bar fills. Then the scene fades in over 0.8 s. Gameplay and input resume when the fade-in starts (`--switch` captures it). Pressing **C** toggles a crouch on the ground (sprinting or jumping cancels it). The crouch is a mechanical tuck sized for one-voxel gaps. The hips drop into a deep squat, the waist telescopes, the head sinks between the shoulders, the antenna retracts, the backpack slides down, the torso leans 46°, the eyes narrow, and footsteps make no dust. The collider is 0.95 tall, and a regression check keeps the visible crouched model under it. Toggling off, or jumping, stands up only if there is headroom for the full collider, so toggling off inside a tunnel stands up automatically at the exit. While crouched in a tight gap the camera swings behind the courier and drops toward the tunnel axis, then eases back afterwards. `--scene crawl` builds a one-voxel-high hole across the spawn lane and creeps through it. Motion lab clip 12 previews the sneak walk.

**Slide.** Pressing crouch while moving faster than 5.8 units/s (a sprint) slides, Apex-style. Pressing crouch in the air at that speed slides on landing. The first slide after a 2.2-second cool-down adds a 4.5-unit/s boost on top of sprint speed (about 12.9 units/s); a slide inside the cool-down keeps its current speed. For 0.3 s after a boost the slide surges: friction is cut to 30%, so the burst carries (heel flames, rushing streaks, violet embers, extra lean-back, a stronger drop-in punch and floor rumble). A slide onto a speed pad becomes a turbo slide: at least 17.5 units/s with a 0.65 s surge. A boosted slide lasts about 1.65 s and covers about 13 units. Outside a surge, friction (3.6 units/s²) plus drag (0.42 per second times the speed) bleed the speed off. The slide faces along its own line. The stick bends it by at most 1.7 rad/s, and pulling back brakes harder. The slide ends at 3.2 units/s or against a wall. At the end the courier stays crouched, or stands straight into a run if sprint is still held and there is headroom. Pressing crouch again mid-slide stands up at speed. Jumping out of a slide keeps the speed through the air: in the air, speed above the current run speed only bleeds off at 1.5 units/s². The slide uses the crouched collider, so it carries through one-voxel gaps. Presentation: the lead (right) leg shoots out straight on its heel with the toe up. The left leg folds under with the knee splayed. The torso leans back with the head level, banked and twisted toward the left hand, which trails on the floor; the right arm reaches ahead. The antenna streams back and the eyes narrow. The drop-in squashes the body and throws dust ahead, a scrape of sparks and a low ring. A boosted slide adds a blue flash, a shell and back-swept rays. While sliding, sparks spray back off the heel, dust boils from both feet and the trailing hand, and scuff marks streak the floor, all scaled by the speed. The camera ducks with a field-of-view punch and a slight roll on the drop-in, widens by up to 10°, and rumbles while sliding. `--scene slide` sprints along the south wall and slides; motion lab clip 14 previews the skid. Pressing sprint mid-slide (the press, not a held key) cancels it straight into a run, keeping at least sprint speed; extra speed carries and bleeds off. Under a low ceiling the cancel waits for headroom. Any stand-up out of a slide pops into the stride: the lead (right) foot lands first, the body lunges forward with a big opposite-arm pump and a short stretch, boots puff blue, and the camera lifts with a field-of-view punch (a sprint cancel adds a ring and shell). The skid pose blends out faster when standing than when staying crouched.

**Lives.** Each level gives three lives, shown as courier heads in a strip under the sector panel. Every destroyed courier costs one: the first two reboot at the last checkpoint as before (the banner says how many are left). The third plays the shutdown, shows "ALL LIVES LOST // RESTARTING SECTOR", fades to black and restarts the level through the black transition, which restores all three. Running out of time still ends the run with the result band.

**Speed pads.** Dark launch strips with lit rails and chevrons that chase along the arrow (courtyard: the north lane heading east, and inside the north-east room heading south through the blast door; corridors: the west corridor heading north, and the east corridor heading south). Stepping on one (unless moving against its arrow) launches the courier along it at 14 units/s or more, keeping 40% of any sideways speed; a slide keeps sliding, faster. Speed above the run speed carries: it bleeds off at 5 units/s² while the stick points ahead, 10 with no input, 1.5 in the air, and pulling against it brakes as usual. The launch fires a flash, ring, shell, back-blasted sparks and light rays, with a camera punch; above sprint speed the courier leans into the wind with both arms swept back, glow trails stream from the boots and backpack, and the view widens by up to 14°.

**Pressure plates and blast doors.** A floor button (amber while sealed, cyan while held, a ring of lamps counting down) opens its split blast door while pressed and for 4 seconds after stepping off; the halves slide into the wall, then seal again, but never on a courier standing in the doorway. Steam vents as a door unlatches and dust kicks off the sill when it slams. Labels show DOOR SEALED / DOOR OPEN with the countdown, plus status strips on the jambs and countdown lamps along the top. Courtyard: a new partition wall south of the north-east room; its plate is in that room and the door is the short way down to the last core and the exit. Corridors: a door in the west wall bypasses the first trap; its plate is at the far south end of the west corridor, with a speed pad on the way back. Both doors are optional shortcuts (the route test treats sealed doors as walls). `--scene boost`, `--scene door` (with `--overview` for the whole level) capture these; `--scene launch` sprints onto the open training-hall pad with the camera behind.

**Speed-pad modes.** How the courier hits a pad decides what it does:
- **Walking: fast stride.** Launched to 9 units/s, with long walking steps at a cadence that matches the speed. The steps lean further and the arms swing 45% bigger. Each step flashes a blue footprint glow, sparks and a little ring, and light streaks trail from the boots. The speed bleeds off at 1.6 units/s², and letting go of the stick ends the stride like a walk. The entry is a lunge with an arm pump.
- **Sprinting (or already flying): low flight.** Launched to 14 units/s or more. A crouch-and-spring take-off lifts the courier into a 0.38-unit hover with a slow bob. The whole body tips about 49° forward about the pelvis and banks into turns. The legs trail together with pointed toes and a small alternating kick, and the arms sweep back and out. Two vivid boot jets burn: a white core with cyan and violet flame, plus violet embers, light rays past the shell and floor dust blown back. The body keeps its proportions in flight, with only a slight 4% stretch along it. The run-hop stretch counts the run's own hop alone, not the hover or trick lift. The flight is timed, not tied to heading. It lasts 2.4 s (a pad refreshes it) whatever the stick does. Steering turns the line in an arc at up to 3.2 rad/s; pulling more than 60° off it air-brakes down to 5 units/s while turning quicker, so even a full reversal stays tight, and the speed builds back along the new line. With no input it holds its line. It hovers over live floor plates (moving hazards still hit). The 2.4 s is a shared boost clock that any pad launch starts (walking, sprinting or sliding) and that runs on its own, whether or not the courier is flying. While it runs, every move stays boosted: a walk or run becomes a stride, a slide keeps surge friction and its surge effects, jumps carry the speed, and the off-pad speed only bleeds away slowly (drag 0.6). Switching moves plays a short whole-body trick about the centre of mass, with a tuck, a lift and a ring of light: a barrel roll (0.42 s) on a dive from flight into a slide, a front flip (0.5 s) on a take-off from a slide and on boosted jumps, and a spin (0.38 s) on a take-off from a run or stride. Out of a slide (boosted or not), standing up plays a kip-up (0.36 s: it rocks back out of the skid, springs up 0.24 and lunges forward into the run; boosted it also spins once), and a jump plays a scissor-kick twist (0.45 s, a full spin with the legs scissored) or, boosted, a corkscrew (0.55 s, a front flip and a full twist at once). These tricks are pose-only and never change speed, heading or collision. While boosted, a jump within 0.16 s of meeting a wall (on the ground or in the air) kicks off it: out at 9 units/s or 85% of the run-in speed, keeping 70% of the speed along the wall, facing away at once, with steering locked for 0.24 s. The wall-kick trick pushes both legs back into the wall, then back-flips while the half turn away from it unwinds. The wall throws a ring of motes across its face, sparks, chips, a flash, a shell and rays, the boots flare into it, and there is a beat of hit-stop. The hop never climbs above a floor jump's apex plus 0.25, and only two kicks are allowed before landing, so walls cannot be climbed. The backpack light strip and a soft halo glow at 55% while sprint is held (standing, running or sliding) and fade on release; during a boost they glow at full strength the whole time. An interaction (core, relay, checkpoint) pauses the flight, and it lifts off again when the interaction ends if time is left. A hit, a wall bounce or a dive into a slide knocks it down (a jump does not), but while time is left a sprint press takes off again (with the take-off move) without restarting the clock. A violet bar along the bottom of the left status panel shows the time left, pulsing while grounded. When the time is up it touches down: the boots swing down into a landing dip with a dust skirt, and the arms swing forward for balance. The camera hums and leans into turns.
- **Sliding: burst.** The slide just gets faster (at least 16 units/s): a white-hot heel spray, a second ring and a fireball. The camera shakes hardest of the three pad hits, with a roll.

Transitions:
- From a stride, pressing sprint takes off into a flight at 12.5 units/s or more.
- From a stride, crouch slides as usual (with the boost when it is ready).
- From a flight, crouch dives into a slide that keeps the flight's speed (no extra boost): a touchdown squash with a slide drop-in.
- Jumping keeps either state: a stride or flight carries on through the air and after landing.
- A hit, pulling back against the motion, or crashing into a wall (which bounces) ends either state.

Extra sparkle and tension:
- **Pads idle:** cyan, violet and pink motes stream along the arrow and rise. They are drawn from time, not spawned, so quiet levels still empty their particles.
- **Every launch:** a detonation: a white-hot core flash and fireball bloom, an impact burst of rays, about 36 tri-colour motes blasting out in every direction and hanging on heavy drag, a floor-skimming spark ring, shards and a violet ground wave, held by a beat of slow motion (0.07 s; 0.035 s for a stride). Also a three-shell sonic-boom cone ahead, tri-colour spark fans, arcs from strip to boots, and a cloud of speed lines rushing past. A stride gets about 60% of this.
- **Flight:**
  - Twin cyan and violet helix ribbons wind around the line of flight, wind streaks rush past, and the touchdown adds a violet ring and a radial spark burst.
  - A flight launch or slide burst adds a 0.05 s beat of slow motion and a dolly zoom: the field of view punches wide while the eye lunges in.
  - While flying, the camera drops lower, trails up to 1.3 units further back, leads further ahead and hums harder.
- **Dust and exhaust.** Slides keep their light effects and add smoke (`Game::smokePuff`: soft grey puffs that swell, swirl and rise). There are heel smoke trails, surge plumes, a rooster tail of smoke at pad speed, and a smoke burst on a pad. A flight's boot jets blast heavy dark dust off the floor, sideways and back, rolling a low cloud behind. Running kicks dust off the heels (denser at a sprint). The sprint exhaust and the boost thrusters breathe grey exhaust puffs as well as flame. Heavy dust (a particle with `extent.x > 0`) renders denser and fades more slowly, so it reads over the light floors.
- **Backpack thrusters:** while any pad boost is left (`Game::boostMeter()`: a flight's clock, a stride's extra speed, or the pad speed a slide, jump or run still carries), twin thrusters on the courier's backpack burn white-core blue flames with sparks and embers, scaled by the meter. A jump with pad speed keeps it carrying after landing (it used to drop to sprint speed at once), so the HUD meter stays up. `--scene slidejump` captures a jump out of a turbo slide.
- **HUD:** above sprint speed, anime-style speed lines rush in from the screen edges (re-dealt 30 times a second), and a flight tints the edges violet.

Motion lab clips 15 (low flight, side view) and 16 (boosted stride) preview them. `--scene flight` and `--scene slidepad` capture the in-game versions. Training hall 3 now has a pad right before its relay-less trap, for flying over it.

**Transition weight.** Moves hand over to each other with weight rather than an even blend (presentation only; nothing changes speed, timing or collision):

- **Tricks** turn on a driven curve: a small wind-up against the turn, a hard snap through it and a slight overshoot that settles. Around each trick the body dips and pulls its arms back as it starts (wind-up), squashes as it lands (plant), and then overshoots along the trick's axis and settles over about a third of a second (follow-through: pitch for flips and kip-ups, bank for the barrel roll, twist for spins), with the head countering and the arms flung through. The body squashes in the wind-up, stretches through the snap and squashes again on the plant. Boosted tricks (the spinning kip-up, the corkscrew, the wall kick, or any trick while boosted) are about 45% stronger, and the spinning kip-up, the corkscrew and a boosted front flip freeze the frame for 0.04 s.
- **Speed pads** whiplash the body: the shove snaps it back for an instant before it whips forward into the rush, head lagging, arms thrown back, and the body squashes then springs long.
- **Slide drop-in** pitches the body forward for an instant before it snaps back into the skid lean (twice as hard on a boosted slide, with a deeper squash). A kip-up plants its first stride with a dip; the boosted one lunges harder.
- **Flight touchdown** lands heavier (landing strength 0.9).
- **Camera:** every trick rolls the view with the turn and lifts it a little; boosted tricks also punch the lens and add a small jolt.

**Interface style.** The whole interface follows the clock card: flat surfaces a shade lighter than the old dark glass (`surface` for large panels, `slate` for cards and tags), hard drop shadows (`shade`) instead of outlines, and motion borrowed from a deck-builder's cards. The tech feel stays in the cut corners, a short accent tick on each panel's top edge, scanlines and the sweeping light on the menu slab, its glowing slanted edge, and the slanted armour cells and progress bars. Every surface is slightly see-through and layered rather than one flat colour: `finishSurface` shades it from a lighter top to a deeper bottom, lays a staggered dot-matrix texture over it with a slow diagonal sheen running through the dots, and adds a thin highlight along the top edge (panels, the menu slab, the clock card, key caps, the hint backing, the result band, the field manual and the transition card). All text uses one rounded face, Arial Rounded MT Bold, read from the Windows font folder at run time (the bundled Rajdhani elsewhere). The pause menu items are one word each: RESUME, REBOOT (back to the last checkpoint or the start), RESTART, SETTINGS, MENU; the title menu's motion lab item is LAB. The level transition and the arrival sequence carry no words: the transition deals a sector card (it flips open, punches in from a settling twist and sways inside a turning ring of ticks; a beacon target for training, a core with one pip per sector for missions) over a segmented load bar whose segments pop as they light; the arrival shows its five boot beats as hex pips that pop in turn, with ENTER and a fast-forward glyph for skipping. Shared helpers in `RelayMenu`: `transformSince` (turn, scale and move everything just drawn), `punch` (overshoot then settle) and `elasticIn` (grow from nothing with an overshoot).

- **Shield break (world):** the bubble flashes white, bursts outward and thins away with bright zig-zag cracks across it; a double ring and a white flash go off, 24 glowing glass shards fly off its surface (harder away from the blow), tumble down, bounce and lie scattered on the floor before fading (Debris with `extent.x > 0`); the frame holds for 0.08 s and the camera shakes lightly, squeezes the lens and dips.
- **HUD:** the cores show as sockets only (no count). A breaking shield shatters like a struck card: a white flash and a swell with a twist, two hex shock rings, sparks, the hex split into twelve spinning wedges, the shield count flashing red, the whole status corner shaking and the screen edges flashing (`--scene shieldbreak` captures it). A hit punches the lost armour cell out in red with a twist, flings red and cyan shards off it and shakes the row; a broken shield bursts into six shards; a recovered core's socket pops with a twist, a hex ring snaps outward, mint shards scatter and the count pops. Banners (messages, reboot, lives lost) are dealt in with an elastic grow from a settling twist and then sway gently. World labels are slate tags with white text that sway slightly, each on its own beat. Key hints are solid caps with dark legends on a lighter backing.
- **Result band:** lighter, on a hard shadow; the title stamps down from twice its size and the stats pop in one by one.
- **Field manual:** a lighter panel, dealt in with a scale overshoot and a twist when it opens.
- **Menus:** the slab is lighter; the VOXEL / RELAY wordmark (and PAUSED) sway and bob on their own beats; the newly selected label pops with a punch and a small twist (title, pause and settings); the UNIT 07 tag and the settings key caps are flat slate.

**Acrylic UI.** Every panel is light grey acrylic with low contrast. Each frame, just before the UI draws, `AppUi::captureBackdrop` halves the rendered scene four times with linear blits; the sixteenth-size copy, magnified with linear filtering, is a soft blur that `drawAcrylicPoly` / `drawAcrylicRect` sample under each panel (mapped to its screen position). Over it go a light grey tint (`slate` for cards and tags, `surface` for large panels; lighter at the top, a cooler grey at the bottom), fine light and dark grain fixed in the panel's frame with a slow sheen through the light specks, a bright top edge, a darker bottom edge, a faint white hairline and a soft shadow. Text is graphite (`graphiteSoft` for secondary) with a light emboss or a soft white glow; selections are white blades; key caps are near-white acrylic with graphite legends; accents (the tick, stripe, amber and status colours) stay small. Bright cyan (`glowCyan`) lights the grey: each sheet has a glowing cyan top edge (a bright core in a soft bloom), a faint cyan hairline and halo, cyan corner brackets, and its grain sparkles cyan where the sheen passes; the menu slab's slanted edge glows cyan, selections carry a glowing cyan underline, and the big titles (VOXEL / RELAY, PAUSED, SETTINGS, the result title, the manual's page title) and the clock's digits are deep teal (`deepTeal`) with a cyan glow (the clock glows amber under 30 s and red under 10 s). The tints themselves are faintly cool. The level transition's card has no backdrop (it sits on black). Covered: banners, world labels, the clock card, key hints, the menu slab, wordmarks and lists, the UNIT 07 tag, settings, the result band, the field manual and the transition card. The top-left status (armour, lives, shield, jetpack) has no backing at all, fully transparent: armour cells lie in recessed graphite slots and glow cyan when full (amber at two cells, red at one, with a pulsing bloom) with a soft bloom and a bright top edge; lives are deep teal heads on a cyan bloom (spent ones fade to graphite); the shield hex glows cyan with a white core, its count in deep teal. The cores at the bottom keep their mint sockets.

**Pickup feedback on the courier.** Taking a power-up flushes the courier with its colour (cyan shield, gold clock, green spring boots): the shell tints and brightens and fades back over about a second, its lights take the colour, a rim of light swells around the body, and two scan rings sweep up it, shaped by the kind (a hexagon for the shield, clock ticks for the clock, a rising coil for the spring boots). The courier answers with a quick flex: chest out, head up, arms flared, elbows bent and fists clenched (`CourierMotion::pickupKind`).

**Clock card (HUD).** The countdown sits on a light cyan, mostly see-through card (white digits over a soft dark shadow so they stay legible) in the top right: rounded, no frame, a hard drop shadow, and self-lit white digits with a soft halo (amber glow under 30 s; red glow under 10 s while the card pulses; an infinity sign in training). Time changes hit it in the style of a deck-builder's scored cards; everything on the card is turned, scaled and moved after drawing. A crimson bite slashes it: a blade streak (white core, crimson edge) sweeps across in 0.06 s, the halves above and below the cut are pushed apart along it and snap back, the card flashes white then crimson and shakes, twists and punches hard, about 40 spark streaks spray off both sides of the cut and 16 card shards fly out and fall, and a frameless "-5 S" (crimson with a dark outline and hard shadow) stamps down from twice its size, wobbles, holds and falls away. A clock pickup pops the card gold and a frameless gold "+30 S" springs up from nothing and floats away.

**Crimson tiles.** A crimson floor area costs 5 s of a mission's countdown on contact (`drainPenalty`), and again every 1 s while the courier stays on crimson (`drainInterval`). Stepping off and straight back on waits out that second too. Jumping or flying over costs nothing, and training (no clock) only flares.

- **Look:** a near-black red bed of dark crimson cells inside a dark-red rim, flickering like a failing light (`crimsonFlicker`: a stepped jitter, brief dropouts toward dark and the odd bright pop; each cell on its own beat, the rim, glow and floor light with the tile's). Above it embers and ash float up: glowing crimson sparks (some brighter) and dark flakes that sway, turn, dim and fade as they rise about 1.7 m (8 to 28 of them, by area).

**Tunnel camera.** The camera switches to a tunnel shot when the courier is crouched or sprinting toward a one-voxel gap (open below, blocked at head height). The look-ahead scales with speed. Each frame the camera finds the least lowering that keeps a clear line from the aim, measured both now and from just inside the gap, and extending at least as far as the eye is now. So a hole in a thin wall only lowers the view part way, while a long tunnel takes it all the way down: pitch toward 0.03, distance toward 2.6, the aim 0.57 lower.
- Lowering is steady over 1.2 s. Arriving fast raises the rate just enough to land before the gap.
- Once less lowering is needed, the view comes back from wherever it is within about 0.22 s, without cutting through the wall.
- While the shot is still lowering into place, the yaw swings behind the courier at 2.6 rad/s (pausing while the player turns the view). Once settled, the mouse turns the view freely, and vertical mouse movement tilts the low view.
- Walls pull the view in steadily during the shot, unless the eye would otherwise end up inside geometry.

The `tunnel_camera` test crawls and slides through a hole. It checks that the view never clips, never jumps more than 0.25 units between frames on the way in, is back to the player's pitch within 0.5 s of leaving, and can be turned with the mouse inside the shot. A running change log is kept in [DEVLOG.md](DEVLOG.md).

**Walls and the camera.** Hugging a wall changes nothing. The line-of-sight test is thin and starts 0.9 units out from the courier, so a wall beside the courier or grazing the line leaves the view alone. Only a wall truly between the view and the courier brings it in, easing in (5/s) and back out (4/s). The eye may never sit inside geometry or outside the room; that boundary (the farthest allowed spot, searched back from the full distance and refined by bisection) caps the distance the view eases toward, so the view glides along walls and never eases past the boundary and snaps back. The line test's hit is bisection-refined too, so the distance changes smoothly as the line sweeps an edge. The view ignores the map boundary: the outer walls count as empty for every camera test, so at the edge the view keeps its distance beyond the wall. The fragment shader then cuts away the boundary wall (and its fixtures) on the side the camera has passed, leaving the floor (`camera_edge` test, `--scene edge`). The `camera_steady` test checks a still courier from many spots and angles in every level for frame-to-frame trembling. A wall bounce gives the camera only a small shake and a lens squeeze. On leaving a gap the tunnel shot ends within about 0.1 s and the view swings straight back up to full distance, adding the least extra pitch that sees over the block just left. That lift settles back to the player's pitch as the courier runs on, and while the gap is within about 3 units behind, that wall does not hold the view back. `--scene tunnel` slides through the training tunnel. The `wall_camera` test walks the courier toward the camera into the courtyard wall, then orbits in the corner, and checks for no clipping and no sudden jumps.

**Field manual.** Training opens a closable, illustrated popup the first time it reaches the checkpoint step or the courier takes a hit. While it is open the game holds and the cursor is free. Its three pages are drawn as vector diagrams:
- **Armour:** hazards break one of five cells, -5 s in missions.
- **Shutdown and reboot:** a downed courier reboots at the beacon with full armour; -10 s and a life in missions.
- **Out of lives:** the sector restarts; running out of time also ends a mission; training is unlimited.

Use NEXT/BACK, Enter/Space and the arrow keys to move between pages; the X, Esc or GOT IT closes it. `--manual N` opens page N for captures.

**Wall bounce.** Running into a wall, closed door or fixture faster than 4.5 units/s (walking speed is 5.2) rebounds: the speed into the wall flips at half strength, sideways speed keeps 85%, and steering waits 0.22 s while the rebound eases off. The courier turns with the rebound during that beat, is knocked upright with its head snapping back and arms thrown up, and the body squashes for a moment. Dust and chips puff at the contact point (sparks above 7 units/s), and the camera jolts back along the rebound. A 0.6 s cool-down stops a held key from bouncing it repeatedly against the same wall: walking back in after a bounce just stops. Moving hazards are excluded, because their blow already knocks the courier back. A slide bounces too and keeps sliding.

**Wall action (left mouse).** It counts when the courier heads at a wall at 45 degrees or more (`wallAngle`; ahead is the way it moves, or faces when still) and the wall is within two cells beyond the body (`wallTrigger`). **Hit types and directions.** Moving hazards deal blunt hits: a 9.5-unit shove, 0.12 s slow-motion hit-stop, the shell dents along the blow, painted shell fragments and a dust cloud burst out, a heavy low-frequency camera kick plays, the screen edges go red, and dizzy stars circle the head for about a second. **Model, run and fall.** The courier is drawn at 80% scale (`Game::modelScale`): about 1.6 units tall, above one voxel. The standing collider follows it (2.10 to 1.70). Shorter legs step 25% faster for the same ground speed, so planted feet still don't slide; the sprint stance is shorter (22% of the stride) to give the swing leg more time. For the same reason, the pose-continuity regression now scales its per-step limit by 1/modelScale (0.22 to 0.275 rad per 1/120 s). It has cuter proportions: a bigger, rounder head on a slightly smaller torso, a glossy dark face screen with tall oval eyes and white catch-lights, a small LED smile that widens when happy and flips to a frown on hits, pink cheek lights that brighten with joy, a crown stripe, round ear discs, a ball-tipped antenna, mitten hands and round boots (still under the collider). On shutdown from a hit, the body is launched away from the blow and tumbles through the air with its limbs trailing toward the source. It lands with a thud, and gravity torque (an inverted pendulum about the feet edge) topples it to lie fully flat (90°, pivoting at the shell's edge so it rests on the floor) with a slap, a small rebound and a line of dust. On the ground the arms lie relaxed along the sides, the legs straighten and the head rolls aside. It then slides to a stop under floor friction, rebounding off walls. A timeout simply tips forward.

**Physics.** Debris and sparks now fall with gravity close to the courier's own (22 and 18 units/s², up from 5 and 11). Debris, sparks and embers collide with the voxel world axis by axis (restitution, floor friction, ricochets off walls, settling on block tops), and dust is pushed out of solids. A shove that meets a wall thuds into it with dust and chips; only the fallen body rebounds, because bouncing a live courier back into a hazard broke the level-route regression.

**Performance.** Frame cost is about 1.5 ms on average and under 4 ms worst case in every capture scene (RTX 4060, 1440x900), and real-time play holds 60 FPS. ## Movement and collision

Movement accelerates to walking speed in about 80 ms and to sprint speed in about 130 ms. Ground braking takes about 60 ms from walking speed; reversing has extra acceleration. Air steering remains available. A held jump reaches about 1.7 units above the take-off point; a quick tap reaches about 1.1. Jump buffering (120 ms) and coyote time (100 ms) make near-edge and near-landing inputs more forgiving without enabling a double jump.

Walking, sprinting, turning, airborne poses, landing compression and hit reactions blend using simulation time. Stride follows actual distance travelled, so pushing into a wall does not keep the legs running at full speed. Interaction turns toward the target smoothly. The model-envelope regression covers running, jumping, reaching, collapsing, banking, landing and hit poses.

Solid collision uses a flat-bottom vertical cylinder, radius 0.70 and height 2.10, with physics substeps no longer than 1/120 second. Floor traps test a separate 0.30-radius foot contact patch against individual visible plates; jumping above the 0.1075-unit plate surface clears the damage zone. Moving hazards use separate body, light-strip and top-lamp boxes shared by physics and rendering. Rounded-corner separation resolves impacts before knockback.

Open **F1 > Show collision shapes**, then close F1 to continue playing with the overlay. Blue rings show the body, yellow shows the foot contact patch, and orange/red boxes show hazard geometry.

On Windows, `GameInput` temporarily disassociates the IME context from this game's own window while focused and not entering text, then restores it on focus loss, text entry or exit. It samples only game controls in the foreground window, clears stale key states on focus changes, and leaves the user's keyboard layout unchanged. Linux/macOS retain GLFW input. The native self-test verifies IME detach/restore and unchanged layout; it does not emulate every installed Chinese IME or replace a manual keyboard pass.
