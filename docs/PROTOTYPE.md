# Voxel Relay - prototype 0.6

Local implementation: C++17, OpenGL 3.3, GLFW and the course starter's ImGui / shader / window / benchmark integration. The original assignment specification remains in the root README. Prototype features were developed with Codex assistance; this document does not attribute the implementation to either student.

## Play

Start `Craft.exe`, select a level and enter the station. Recover every core with **E**, then pass through the unlocked gate. Walking into a core does not collect it.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move relative to the camera |
| Mouse / wheel | Orbit / zoom |
| Hold either Shift | Sprint, 8.4 units/s instead of 5.2 |
| Space | Tap for a low jump; hold for full height |
| E | Recover the nearest core or operate a relay |
| Tab | Release cursor and pause simulation |
| Escape | Pause menu / resume |
| R / 1 / 2 | Restart / courtyard / corridors |
| F1 / F2 / F3 | Tools / overview camera / motion workshop |

Mouse sensitivity is 0.0016 radians per pixel (previously 0.0035). The courier has five armour cells. A valid hazard hit removes one cell and five seconds, launches the courier away, and grants 1.1 seconds of damage immunity. Static walls block movement. The fifth hit triggers shutdown, a collapse animation, fading lights and sparks; restart restores the entire level. Timer expiry also causes shutdown.

Core recovery is a 0.46-second reach animation with an energy tether. The core is awarded halfway through the animation, once only. A hit interrupts unfinished recovery. Switches use the same interaction key and temporarily disable their linked floor trap.

## Test facility update (0.6)

The default art direction is a quiet research facility: warm white shells, graphite structural parts, blue status lights and amber hazard accents. The courier now has curved ceramic armour, rounded shoulder bearings, exposed leg pistons, recessed optical modules and serial markings. Spheres, cylinders and bevelled panels are batched by primitive. The room has observation-window panels, linear lighting, sector numbers and floor guidance; core and relay housings follow the same palette. All models and materials are generated by project code. F1 still offers the whitebox colour mode for diagnostics.

The main menu opens onto a maintenance bay. **Begin Reactivation / Enter** starts a four-second arrival scene: lights and eyes turn on, the courier raises its head, checks both sides, extends its limbs and stands, then the camera returns behind it. **Enter or Space** skips this sequence. Gameplay controls, hazards and the countdown wait until the sequence ends; losing focus or pausing also pauses its clock. The orbit checks scene geometry to avoid travelling through walls.

Turning uses a bounded angular velocity with acceleration and settling, so a reversal has a visible rotation rather than a rapid orientation snap. Head aim leads torso twist and foot realignment. Running adds counter-rotation, asymmetric arm recovery and head stabilisation. Braking holds the weight shift briefly before recovering; landing compression and hold duration depend on impact speed, with a small camera dip. A seven-unit fall is heavier than an ordinary jump. These presentation effects keep the existing jump and movement inputs responsive.

The F3 motion lab includes **High drop / impact hold** (9) and **Reactivation / wake** (10), alongside the previous nine clips. Public visual references: Valve's [Portal 2 media page](https://www.thinkwithportals.com/media.php) and [official Steam screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/620/Portal_2/). These informed the material contrast and industrial presentation; the project contains no imported Valve game assets.

Capture the arrival with `Craft --intro --frames 258 --record frames/arrival`, or a hard landing with `Craft --scene drop --frames 46 --capture impact.png`.

## Expressive motion update (0.5)

The courier now uses a shared pelvis rig and two-link leg IK with forward-bending knees. The support foot moves against the body's travel during each stance, while the other foot lifts, swings and rolls onto its next step. Gait phase follows actual distance; torso counter-rotation, spring-driven lean and banking, head lead and antenna follow-through add movement without delaying controls.

Starts, braking and reversals have short settling poses. Jumping blends into asymmetrical knee tuck, then extends the feet before impact; landing compression scales with impact speed and rebounds before settling. Contact shadows follow the floor below the robot. The collider dimensions and input timings remain the same as 0.4.

Recovery separates reaching, finger closure, retraction and a small fist-pump celebration. The face blinks, reacts to speed and hits, opens its eyes in the air, and shows happy eyes after recovery and at the exit. Idle behaviour includes looking around and checking a wrist. Hits drive directional recoil; shutdown slumps the body and fades its lights. Victory continues animating while the completed level's timer is frozen.

Press **F3** to open the motion workshop and choose Jog, Sprint, Jump, Brake, Pivot, Idle, Recover, Hurt, Victory, High drop or Reactivation. Press F3 again to return to the level. These previews use the same pose builder as gameplay, with deterministic demonstration signals. Public references and the independent implementation choices are documented in [ANIMATION_REFERENCE.md](ANIMATION_REFERENCE.md).

## Movement and collision

Movement accelerates to walking speed in about 80 ms and to sprint speed in about 130 ms. Ground braking takes about 60 ms from walking speed; reversing has extra acceleration. Air steering remains available. A held jump reaches about 1.7 units above the take-off point; a quick tap reaches about 1.1. Jump buffering (120 ms) and coyote time (100 ms) make near-edge and near-landing inputs more forgiving without enabling a double jump.

Walking, sprinting, turning, airborne poses, landing compression and hit reactions blend using simulation time. Stride follows actual distance travelled, so pushing into a wall does not keep the legs running at full speed. Interaction turns toward the target smoothly. The model-envelope regression covers running, jumping, reaching, collapsing, banking, landing and hit poses.

Solid collision uses a flat-bottom vertical cylinder, radius 0.70 and height 2.10, with physics substeps no longer than 1/120 second. Floor traps test a separate 0.30-radius foot contact patch against individual visible plates; jumping above the 0.1075-unit plate surface clears the damage zone. Moving hazards use separate body, light-strip and top-lamp boxes shared by physics and rendering. Rounded-corner separation resolves impacts before knockback.

Open **F1 > Show collision shapes**, then close F1 to continue playing with the overlay. Blue rings show the body, yellow shows the foot contact patch, and orange/red boxes show hazard geometry.

On Windows, `GameInput` temporarily disassociates the IME context from this game's own window while focused and not entering text, then restores it on focus loss, text entry or exit. It samples only game controls in the foreground window, clears stale key states on focus changes, and leaves the user's keyboard layout unchanged. Linux/macOS retain GLFW input. The native self-test verifies IME detach/restore and unchanged layout; it does not emulate every installed Chinese IME or replace a manual keyboard pass.

## Implementation map

- `src/core/RelayGame.*`: physics substeps, collision, five-hit health, knockback, sprinting, interaction, particles and level state.
- `src/core/Collision.h`: rounded cylinder/box narrow-phase collision.
- `src/core/ArrivalSequence.h`: focus-aware, skippable arrival clock, independent of gameplay time.
- `src/core/CourierMotion.h`: presentation signals and damped springs, driven by gameplay events.
- `src/graphics/GameInput.*`, `src/core/ButtonState.h`: window-scoped IME handling, foreground controls and focus-safe key edges.
- `src/core/FollowCamera.*`: camera basis, orbit, smoothing and obstruction checks.
- `src/graphics/RelayModels.*`: editable parent-child robot / gate models. Robot details include helmet, reactor, shoulder armour, elbows, hands, fingers, knees, boots and backpack cooling fins.
- `src/graphics/CourierPose.cpp`: shared pelvis, foot trajectories, leg IK, layered reactions, facial expression and deterministic motion previews.
- `src/graphics/RelayRenderer.*`: voxel face removal, bevelled model primitives, instanced rendering and procedural material textures.
- `src/app/RelayMenu.*`, `AppUi.*`: menu, HUD and diagnostics.
- `tests/relay_tests.cpp`: gameplay regressions and animated model-envelope checks.

## Build and verification

Standard CMake workflow (C++17 compiler, Make, Python + Jinja2, and platform OpenGL/window dependencies required):

```sh
cmake -S . -B build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release
cmake --build build --parallel
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure
```

This machine uses `E:/work/MiniCraft/tools/w64devkit/bin`, with its build in `E:/CodexWorkspace/.cache/temp/voxel-relay-build`. Existing course dependency source caches are reused through CMake's `FETCHCONTENT_SOURCE_DIR_*` options.

All 37 registered checks pass on Windows, including both complete level routes, variable-height jumping, jump buffering, coyote time, 30/60/144 FPS consistency, trap boundaries, compound mover collision, pose continuity, focus transitions and native IME restoration. The motion checks cover support-foot planting, gameplay animation events and visible-part bounds across all eleven clips. Version 0.6 adds bounded turning, impact-strength differentiation and awakening/skip/focus checks. Windows / RTX 4060 rendering has been checked for jump, sprint, pickup, trap-edge and death scenes. VLAB/Linux execution still needs verification.

Input restoration check: `Craft --input-self-test` (Windows).

Motion capture: `Craft --gallery --motion 1 --frames 96 --record frames/sprint`. Motion IDs 0-10 follow the workshop order above. Recording writes PNG frames at 30 fps from a fixed 60 Hz simulation and omits the workshop overlay; it is intended for inspecting animation, not measuring real-time performance.

Reproducible captures: `Craft --scene pickup --frames 15 --capture pickup.png`, `--scene knockback`, `--scene death`, `--scene sprint`, `--scene trap-edge --colliders`, `--menu` or `--gallery`. `CRAFT_BENCHMARK=1` freezes the level and uses the overview camera at 1920x1080, 60 warm-up frames and 240 measured frames; compare with `--baseline`. ImGui, game simulation and initial mesh creation are outside the scene timer. Both comparison modes use the same model geometry and effects.
